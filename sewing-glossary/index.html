Sewing, knitting and hosiery glossary

The terms you will meet in manuals, patterns and the hosiery trade, explained simply.

Photo: Sara Bach on Unsplash

On this page

  1. A to F
  2. G to O
  3. P to Z
  4. Need help with a machine?

A to F

Bias
The diagonal direction across woven fabric, which has the most stretch.
Bobbin
The small spool that holds the lower thread under the needle plate.
Bobbin case
Holder for the bobbin in front-loading machines, with its own tension screw.
Coverstitch
Parallel rows of stitching on top with a looped chain underneath, used for knit hems.
Denier
Fibre weight in grams per 9,000 metres; in tights, lower is sheerer.
Differential feed
Two feed dogs moving at different speeds to stop knits stretching or fine fabric puckering.
Feed dogs
The toothed bars under the foot that move fabric along.
Framework knitting
Knitting on a hand-operated stocking frame, the origin of the hosiery trade.
Fully fashioned
Knitwear knitted to shape and then linked, rather than cut from fabric.

G to O

Hook
The rotating part that catches the needle thread to form a lockstitch.
Hosier
A maker or seller of hosiery.
Interfacing
A layer that adds structure to collars, cuffs and facings.
Latch needle
The needle type used in most knitting machines.
Linking
Joining knitted pieces loop to loop, used in fully fashioned knitwear and sock toes.
Lockstitch
The standard machine stitch, formed by needle and bobbin threads interlocking.
Looper
The parts in an overlocker that carry the lower threads.
Overlocker (serger)
A machine that seams, trims and neatens an edge in one pass.

P to Z

Presser foot
Holds fabric against the feed dogs; many specialist feet exist.
Selvedge
The finished edge of woven fabric that doesn’t fray.
Servo motor
A quiet, efficient motor for industrial machines with fine speed control.
Shank
The way feet attach: low, high or slant shank, or snap-on.
Tension
The balance between upper and lower threads that makes an even stitch.
Throat space
The distance from the needle to the machine body, important for quilts.
Walking foot
A foot with its own feed teeth that moves layers evenly.
Wadding (batting)
The middle layer of a quilt.
