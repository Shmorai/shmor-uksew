Starting an alterations business: the equipment checklist

Hems, zips and take-ins pay the bills. Here is the kit an alterations or tailoring business needs to work quickly and well.

By the UK SEW editorial team · 1 October 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Core machines

  • Industrial or heavy-duty lockstitch: the main machine for most jobs.
  • Overlocker: to neaten seams after taking in.
  • Blind hem machine: fast, invisible trouser and skirt hems.
  • Domestic zigzag machine: for stretch fabrics and buttonholes if your lockstitch is straight-stitch only.

Pressing

A good steam iron or steam generator, a pressing table, a tailor’s ham, sleeve board and pressing cloths make finished work look professional.

Cutting and marking

Sharp shears, snips, a seam ripper, tailor’s chalk, a long ruler and a cutting table.

Fitting and front of house

  • A full-length mirror and changing space.
  • Pins, tape measures and a pinning cushion.
  • A booking system and garment tags.

Buy new or used?

Used industrial machines from a reputable dealer can stretch a start-up budget a long way. Make sure each has been serviced, comes with the right motor and table, and that parts are easy to get.

Pricing your work fairly

Work out your hourly cost (rent, power, equipment, your time) and price jobs from typical times: hems, zips, take-ins and so on. Keep a price list visible, and quote before starting complex work.

Keep machines earning

Downtime loses money. Schedule services in quieter weeks, keep spares, and have an engineer you can call quickly.

Common jobs and the kit they need

JobMain equipment
Trouser and skirt hemsBlind hem machine or lockstitch, pressing
Zip replacementLockstitch with zipper foot
Taking in and letting outLockstitch, overlocker
Jeans hemsHeavy-duty or industrial lockstitch, jeans needles
Knitwear repairsOverlocker, coverstitch or domestic zigzag

Spares and support

Keep spare needles, bobbins, belts and feet in stock, and know who will service your machines quickly. Frequently asked questions

What sewing machine do I need for alterations?
Most alteration businesses use an industrial or heavy-duty lockstitch, an overlocker and often a blind hem machine, plus good pressing equipment.
