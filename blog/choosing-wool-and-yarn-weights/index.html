Wool and yarn weights explained

UK and US patterns use different names for the same yarns. Here is a simple guide to yarn weights and how to choose.

By the UK Sew editorial team · 27 September 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: June Gathercole on Unsplash

On this page

  1. The main UK weights
  2. Read the ball band
  3. Knit a tension square
  4. From wool to hosiery

The main UK weights

  • 4-ply (US fingering): socks, baby wear, fine knits. Around 2.75 to 3.25mm needles.
  • Double knit / DK (US light worsted): the all-rounder. Around 4mm needles.
  • Aran (close to US worsted): cables and warm jumpers. Around 4.5 to 5mm needles.
  • Chunky (US bulky): quick, cosy projects. Around 6 to 8mm needles.

Needle sizes are typical ranges; always follow the ball band and your pattern.

Read the ball band

The band shows fibre content, weight, length, recommended needle size, tension (stitches and rows per 10cm) and washing instructions. Buy enough from the same dye lot for the whole project.

Knit a tension square

Knit a 10cm square before you start. If you have more stitches than the pattern, go up a needle size; fewer, go down.

From wool to hosiery

Sock yarns, often wool blended with nylon for strength, are a direct link to the East Midlands hosiery tradition. Read Leicester’s hosiery heritage. And if you sew as well as knit, PK Sewing Machines in Leicester can service your machine. See our wool and yarn guide.
