How to get the right part first time

  1. Find the make and model number, usually on a plate on the back or underside of the machine.
  2. For needles, check the needle system in the manual or on the old needle packet (for example 130/705H for most domestic machines).
  3. For bobbins, compare against the old one, or take it with you. Similar-looking bobbins are not interchangeable.
  4. For feet, check whether your machine is low shank, high shank or slant shank, or uses snap-on feet.
  5. If in doubt, take the machine or part to a specialist.

Genuine vs compatible parts

Compatible parts can be good value for consumables like bobbins and feet, but for timing-critical parts such as hooks and loopers, quality matters. Poorly made hooks can cause skipped stitches and damage.

Parts for vintage machines

Older Singer, Jones, Frister & Rossmann and similar machines are still repairable. Common replacements are belts, bobbin winder tyres, motor brushes, light bulbs and feet. Some mechanical parts are now sourced second-hand or as reproductions, which is where a dealer’s trade contacts help.

Keep a small spares kit

  • A pack of needles in your two most-used sizes, plus ballpoint.
  • Spare bobbins (the correct class).
  • Small screwdriver and lint brush.
  • Spare light bulb or LED for older machines.

Frequently asked questions

Are all sewing machine needles the same?
No. Most domestic machines use the 130/705H system, but industrial machines use other systems such as DBx1 or DCx27. Sizes and point types also vary by fabric.
Where can I find parts for an old Singer?
Specialist dealers with large parts stocks, such as PK Sewing Machines in Leicester, can often supply or source parts for older machines. Have the model number ready.
