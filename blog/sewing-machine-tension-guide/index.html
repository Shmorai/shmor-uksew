Sewing machine tension made simple

Tension is the balance between the top and bobbin threads. Get it right and stitches look perfect on both sides.

By the UK Sew editorial team · 3 October 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Ashley Diane Worsham on Unsplash

What a balanced stitch looks like

In a balanced lockstitch, the two threads interlock in the middle of the fabric layers. You see only top thread on top and only bobbin thread underneath. Use contrasting colours on a test scrap to check.

Rethread first

Most “tension problems” are threading problems. Raise the presser foot (which opens the tension discs) and rethread the top completely. Check the bobbin is inserted the right way round.

Adjusting top tension

  • Top thread visible underneath: increase top tension a little.
  • Bobbin thread visible on top: decrease top tension a little.
  • Change one step at a time and test.

Bobbin tension

On front-loading machines the bobbin case has a small screw. Adjust only in tiny turns, and only if top tension changes don’t help. Many drop-in systems shouldn’t be adjusted at home.

Fabric and thread matter

Heavy thread, thick layers and stretch fabrics all change how tension behaves. Match needle size to thread and fabric, and test on the actual fabric you are sewing.

A tension test you can repeat

  1. Thread a different colour on top and in the bobbin.
  2. Sew a line of straight stitch and a line of zigzag on two layers of medium cotton.
  3. Check both sides. Adjust the top dial one step and sew again.
  4. Note the best setting for that fabric.

Tension on industrial machines

Industrial machines have a similar top tension assembly and a bobbin case with a tension spring. Operators often set bobbin tension by the “drop test”, but follow the manufacturer’s guidance or ask your engineer.

When to call a professional

If tension won’t balance after rethreading, a new needle and small adjustments, the machine may need a service. PK Sewing Machines in Leicester services all major makes. Leicester’s engineers have kept machines running since the days of the great hosiery mills.

Frequently asked questions

What number should my sewing machine tension be on?
Many machines have a default mark (often around 4 on a 0 to 9 dial) for general sewing. Adjust from there for different fabrics and threads.
