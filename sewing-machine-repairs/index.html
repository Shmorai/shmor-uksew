Sewing machine repairs and servicing

A regular service keeps stitches even and prevents expensive breakdowns. Here is what is involved and where to go in Leicester.

Photo: Lidya Nada on Unsplash

What a typical service covers

  1. Inspect & test

    Check stitch quality, noise and any reported faults before work starts.

  2. Clean & oil

    Remove lint and old oil from the hook area, feed dogs and moving parts, then lubricate.

  3. Adjust

    Set tension, timing and feed so stitches form correctly.

  4. Test & explain

    Sew test seams and explain any parts that need replacing.

How often to service

As a rule of thumb, home machines in regular use benefit from a service every year or so, and sooner if stitch quality changes. Industrial machines in daily production need daily care and more frequent professional checks. See how often to service a sewing machine.

Signs your machine needs attention

  • Skipped stitches or looping underneath.
  • Thread bunching (“bird’s nesting”) under the fabric.
  • Knocking, grinding or a much louder motor.
  • Fabric not feeding evenly.
  • Needles breaking often.

Start with our common sewing machine problems guide. Many faults are fixed by rethreading and a new needle.

Before you bring a machine in

  • Note the fault: when it happens, on what fabric, and any noises.
  • Bring the foot pedal, power cable, bobbin case and a bobbin.
  • Include a sample of the stitching problem if you can.
  • Ask for a quote before work starts.

Repair or replace?

Repair usually makes sense for a well-built machine with available parts. Replacement makes sense when a budget machine needs an expensive electronic board, or parts no longer exist. A good engineer will tell you honestly.
