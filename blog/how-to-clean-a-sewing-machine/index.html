How to clean a sewing machine

Ten minutes of cleaning keeps stitches smooth and your machine quiet. Here is how to do it safely.

By the UK SEW editorial team · 10 October 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: J Williams on Unsplash

On this page

  1. What you need
  2. Step by step
  3. What not to do
  4. Cleaning vintage machines
  5. Cleaning overlockers
  6. How often to clean
  7. Signs your machine needs more than a clean
  8. When to book a service

What you need

  • A small lint brush (often supplied with the machine).
  • A small screwdriver for the needle plate.
  • Sewing machine oil, if your manual says to oil.
  • A soft cloth.

Step by step

  1. Unplug the machine.
  2. Remove the needle, presser foot and needle plate.
  3. Remove the bobbin and bobbin case (if removable).
  4. Brush lint from the feed dogs, hook and bobbin area.
  5. Wipe away dust and old oil.
  6. Oil only where the manual shows, one drop at a time.
  7. Reassemble, fit a new needle and sew on scrap to absorb excess oil.

What not to do

  • Don’t blow into the machine or use canned air, which can push lint deeper.
  • Don’t use household oils or WD-40.
  • Don’t take apart electronics or the motor.

Cleaning vintage machines

Older all-metal machines often have many oil points, marked in the manual. Remove old oil and fluff carefully, then oil each point sparingly. Don’t use harsh solvents on decals and paintwork.

Cleaning overlockers

Open the front and side covers, brush out the lint around the loopers and knives, and oil only where the manual says. Overlockers shed more lint than sewing machines, so clean them more often.

How often to clean

Brush out lint after every project, or every few hours of sewing, and immediately after sewing fleece, felt, flannel or wadding. Wipe the outside regularly and keep the machine covered. A deeper clean under the needle plate is worth doing monthly if you sew often.

Signs your machine needs more than a clean

  • It still sounds rough or knocks after cleaning.
  • The handwheel feels stiff.
  • Stitches skip even with a new needle and correct threading.
  • The motor is slow or smells hot.

These are signs it is time for a professional service.

When to book a service

If the machine is still noisy, stiff or stitching badly after cleaning, it’s time for a professional service. PK Sewing Machines services domestic and industrial machines in Leicester, part of the city’s long textile tradition. See how often to service.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use compressed air to clean my sewing machine?
It is best avoided. Compressed air can push lint deeper into the machine and moisture into parts. Use a brush instead.
