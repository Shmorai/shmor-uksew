Sewing machine hire vs buy

For a one-off project, hire can make sense. For anything longer, buying, especially used, often wins. Here is how to decide.

By the UK SEW editorial team · 24 September 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

When hire wins

  • One project with a clear end date.
  • Trying a machine type before buying.
  • A short-term production spike for a small business.

When buying wins

If you will sew for more than a few months, the cost of hire adds up quickly. A tested used machine keeps much of its value, so you can sell it or part-exchange it later.

Questions to ask a hire company

  • What is the total cost, including deposit and delivery?
  • Has the machine been serviced and safety-checked?
  • What happens if it breaks?
  • Are needles, bobbins and a manual included?

A worked example (without made-up prices)

Compare three numbers before you decide: the total hire cost for the period you need, the price of a serviced used machine, and its likely resale or part-exchange value afterwards. If the hire cost is close to the difference between buying and reselling, buying usually wins.

Hire for businesses

For a small business, hire or short-term arrangements can cover a seasonal peak or a one-off contract without a long-term commitment. Check who services the machine, how quickly faults are fixed, and whether hire payments can count towards buying.

Buying used and reselling

Quality used machines tend to hold their value if kept clean and serviced. Keep the manual, accessories and service receipts: they help when you sell or part-exchange later.

Hire checklist to keep

  • Machine make, model and serial number on the agreement.
  • Photos of condition on delivery.
  • Accessories supplied: feet, bobbins, needles, manual, pedal.
  • Contact for faults and expected response time.
  • Return date and condition expected.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to rent or buy a sewing machine?
For a few weeks, renting can be cheaper. For longer periods, buying a used machine and reselling it later is often better value.
