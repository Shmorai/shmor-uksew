Is a “deal” really a deal?

  • Compare the total cost: delivery, a service and any missing accessories.
  • A machine that needs a £50+ repair is not a bargain at a low price.
  • Check parts availability for the model before you buy.
  • Ask what testing, warranty or returns the seller offers.

When to buy

  • Before the school year and after The Great British Sewing Bee, demand for beginner machines rises; compare early.
  • Workroom clearances can release good used industrial machines.
  • Craft shows sometimes have exhibitor offers, but compare with a local dealer who will service the machine later.

Buying from a business vs a private seller

In the UK, goods bought from a business must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. Private sales give you far fewer rights, so inspect and test carefully. Note that PKSM states its used machines are tested on its own bench before sale and sold as seen, so ask about the terms on any machine you are interested in.
