How to choose a sewing machine

Too many stitches, not enough guidance? Here is a calm, practical way to choose a sewing machine that suits what you actually sew.

By the UK Sew editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Kemal Berkay Dogan on Unsplash

  2. Step 2: pick a machine type
  3. Step 3: features that genuinely help

Step 1: list what you will sew

Write down your three most likely projects. Mending and alterations, dressmaking with woven cottons, stretchy jersey, quilting, bags and upholstery all ask different things of a machine. The heaviest fabric on your list sets the minimum standard.

Step 2: pick a machine type

  • Mechanical: simple, durable and easy to repair. Ideal for beginners.
  • Computerised: more stitches, automatic buttonholes, needle up/down and speed control.
  • Heavy-duty: stronger motor for denim and canvas.
  • Overlocker: a second machine for neat seams on knits, not a replacement.

Compare them on our sewing machines page.

Step 3: features that genuinely help

  • A reliable one-step buttonhole.
  • Adjustable stitch length and width.
  • A free arm for sleeves and trouser hems.
  • Presser foot pressure control.
  • Standard 130/705H needles and easy-to-find bobbins.

Step 4: decide new or used

A good used machine that has been serviced and tested by a specialist is often better made than a budget new one. Our used machine checklist explains what to look for.

Step 5: test-sew before you buy

Take your own fabric to a dealer and try straight stitch, zigzag and a buttonhole. In Leicester, PK Sewing Machines has sold new and used machines since 1982, close to the streets where Leicester’s hosiery trade once made stockings for the world.
