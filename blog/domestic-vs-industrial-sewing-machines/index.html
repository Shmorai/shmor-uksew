Domestic vs industrial sewing machines

Thinking of going industrial for a small business or a heavy hobby? Here is how the two worlds differ.

By the UK SEW editorial team · 10 October 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Built for different jobs

A domestic machine does many things reasonably well: straight stitch, zigzag, buttonholes, decorative stitches. An industrial machine usually does one job extremely well, all day, at high speed. That is why workrooms have separate lockstitch, overlock and coverstitch machines.

Speed and control

Industrial machines sew far faster than domestic ones. Modern servo motors make them controllable at low speeds too, which is why many home sewers now use them.

Space, weight and noise

An industrial machine comes on its own table with a motor underneath. It needs floor space and is heavier to move. Servo models are much quieter than old clutch motors.

Running costs

Industrial machines use their own needle systems (for example DBx1 for many lockstitch machines) and need regular oiling unless they are dry-head models. Parts are widely available through trade suppliers.

Side by side

DomesticIndustrial
StitchesMany, on one machineUsually one type per machine
SpeedModerateVery high, controllable with servo motors
Needles130/705H (flat shank)Round shank systems such as DBx1, DCx27
Set-upPortable, on any tableFixed table and motor
MaintenancePeriodic serviceDaily oiling (unless dry-head) and regular checks

Semi-industrial and heavy-duty options

Between the two sit heavy-duty domestic machines and portable “semi-industrial” straight-stitch machines. They suit home sewers who want speed and power without a full industrial table.

Who should consider an industrial machine?

  • Small businesses: alterations, curtains, upholstery, bags and clothing makers.
  • Home sewers who sew a lot of straight seams and want speed and consistency.
  • Anyone working regularly with heavy fabrics, many layers or leather (choose a walking foot model).

If you mostly sew a variety of small projects, buttonholes and decorative stitches, a good domestic machine is usually more practical.

Noise and space at home

Clutch motors spin all the time and hum; servo motors only run when you press the pedal, so they are much quieter and better for homes. An industrial table is roughly a desk in size. Check the room has a sturdy floor, good light and a nearby socket.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use an industrial sewing machine at home?
Yes, many people do, especially with a quiet servo motor. Check space, power and that you have support for servicing and parts.
