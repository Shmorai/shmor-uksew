Start with what you will sew

The right machine depends on the work. Light dressmaking, mending and school projects need very different things from upholstery, bag-making or a small alterations business. Write down the three fabrics you sew most, then look for a machine that handles the heaviest of them comfortably.

Features worth paying for

  • A good one-step buttonhole if you make garments.
  • Adjustable presser foot pressure for knits and layered fabrics.
  • Needle up/down and speed control, which make curves and corners easier.
  • A metal internal frame for stability and longevity.
  • Common parts: a machine that takes standard 15x1 (130/705H) needles and widely available bobbins is cheaper to run.

Budget bands: what you get

We don’t quote prices because they change constantly, but machines fall into clear bands:

  • Entry level: mechanical machines with a handful of stitches, a four-step or simple buttonhole and plastic housings. Fine for mending and occasional projects.
  • Mid range: the sweet spot for most home sewers, with a one-step buttonhole, needle up/down, more feet in the box and a sturdier frame.
  • Premium: larger throat space for quilting, automatic thread cutting, knee lifters, superior feed systems and dealer support.
  • Used and serviced: often the best value of all, especially older all-metal machines.

New or used?

A used machine that has been serviced and bench-tested by a specialist is often better value than a budget new model. Older all-metal machines can run for decades with regular servicing. See our used sewing machine checklist, and ask a dealer what testing and returns they offer.

Try before you buy

Sewing machines feel different in use. If you can, visit a dealer, bring your own fabric and test straight stitch, zigzag and a buttonhole. In Leicester and the East Midlands, PK Sewing Machines can advise on domestic, overlocker and embroidery machines.

Throat space, weight and portability

The throat space (the gap to the right of the needle) matters for quilts and coats. Heavier machines vibrate less at speed; lighter ones are easier to take to classes. If you will carry it, look for a carry handle and a hard cover.

Getting the most from a new machine

  • Read the manual and sew a sampler of every stitch on scrap fabric.
  • Keep a notebook of settings that worked for each fabric.
  • Buy spare needles, bobbins and a lint brush on day one.
  • Book a service after the first heavy year of use.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best sewing machine for a beginner?
A sturdy mechanical or simple computerised machine from a well-supported brand, with an easy one-step buttonhole and widely available parts. Avoid toy-grade mini machines.
Can a domestic machine sew denim and leather?
Many can handle denim with the right needle (a jeans needle, size 100/16) and slower speed. Thick leather and upholstery usually need a heavy-duty or industrial machine.
