British heritage · since 1589

Four centuries of British sewing and knitting

The stocking frame was invented in Nottinghamshire in 1589, and the East Midlands went on to knit stockings and socks for the world. The Scottish Borders became famous for knitwear, the Outer Hebrides for hand-woven Harris Tweed, and Wales for its woollen mills.

UK SEW brings that know-how to today’s home sewers, tailors and workrooms, wherever they are in Britain.

Britain’s textile heritage

Britain built much of the modern textile world. Framework knitting began in Nottinghamshire, and Leicester and Nottingham became the centres of the hosiery trade, home to firms such as Corah and Wolsey. Scotland, Yorkshire, Lancashire and Wales all have their own proud traditions in wool, weaving and knitwear. Explore the story of British hosiery and our UK sector reading list.

Hosiery, knitwear and the machines behind them

Hosiery means knitted legwear and underwear: socks, tights and stockings. Today’s socks are knitted on circular machines and finished on linking and toe-closing machines, while garments are sewn on overlockers and coverstitch machines, the same types that keep Leicester’s workrooms busy. Read our hosiery and knitwear guide, what denier means and how socks are made.

Frequently asked questions

Is UK SEW a shop?
No. UK SEW is a free, independent guide to sewing machines, parts, wool and services in the UK. When you buy, you buy directly from the seller.
Where can I get my sewing machine serviced?
Most areas have independent sewing machine repairers; ask about their experience with your make and whether they quote first. Our recommended supplier, PK Sewing Machines, services domestic and industrial machines and lists servicing from £35.
Do your reviews include customer star ratings?
No. Our reviews are editorial overviews of brands and machine types. We do not publish invented ratings or customer reviews.
