How Leicester clothed the world

For three centuries, Leicester’s knitters made stockings, underwear and knitwear worn around the world. Here is the story in brief.

By the UK Sew editorial team · 4 October 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Thái An on Unsplash

On this page

  1. A frame in every cottage
  2. The factory age
  3. The great names

A frame in every cottage

The stocking frame, credited to William Lee of Calverton in 1589, slowly spread through the East Midlands. By the early 1700s Leicester had several hundred frames, and by 1790 around 3,000. Whole families worked at home, often in hard conditions and for low pay.

The factory age

From the mid-1800s, steam power and new machines moved production into factories. By the end of the century nearly all knitted goods were factory-made. Leicester’s skyline filled with hosiery works.

The great names

N. Corah & Sons built St Margaret’s Works from 1865, one of the largest hosiery factories in Britain. Wolsey, whose roots go back to an 18th-century Leicester hosier, took its name in 1897 from Cardinal Wolsey, buried at Leicester Abbey. Read more in our Corah story.

Change and continuity

Late 20th-century global competition closed many big mills. But Leicester still has knitters, garment makers and dyers, and a strong base of machinery engineers and suppliers.

Keeping the machines running

Firms like PK Sewing Machines, family-run in Leicester since 1982, have supported the city’s workrooms and home sewers for over four decades. Explore our full Leicester hosiery heritage page.
