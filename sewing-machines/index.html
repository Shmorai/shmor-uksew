Start with what you will sew

The right machine depends on the work. Light dressmaking, mending and school projects need very different things from upholstery, bag-making or a small alterations business. Write down the three fabrics you sew most, then look for a machine that handles the heaviest of them comfortably.

Features worth paying for

  • A good one-step buttonhole if you make garments.
  • Adjustable presser foot pressure for knits and layered fabrics.
  • Needle up/down and speed control, which make curves and corners easier.
  • A metal internal frame for stability and longevity.
  • Common parts: a machine that takes standard 15x1 (130/705H) needles and widely available bobbins is cheaper to run.

New or used?

A used machine that has been serviced and bench-tested by a specialist is often better value than a budget new model. Older all-metal machines can run for decades with regular servicing. See our used sewing machine checklist, and ask a dealer what testing and returns they offer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best sewing machine for a beginner?
A sturdy mechanical or simple computerised machine from a well-supported brand, with an easy one-step buttonhole and widely available parts. Avoid toy-grade mini machines.
Can a domestic machine sew denim and leather?
Many can handle denim with the right needle (a jeans needle, size 100/16) and slower speed. Thick leather and upholstery usually need a heavy-duty or industrial machine.
